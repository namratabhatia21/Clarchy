      1Write the brief

      Describe the app in a few plain sentences, or attach a Word, PDF, Excel, Markdown or text file of up to 10 MB. Tables are read as well as paragraphs. Each fact below changes the design; anything you leave out becomes an assumption or a question you can answer later.

      Example brief

      An assistant that answers employees' questions from our HR policy documents, for about 8,000 employees in Germany. Employees sign in with SSO. It needs 99.9% uptime, GDPR applies, and chat history must be kept for 2 years. The budget is $4,000 a month, and our 6 developers already use GitHub Copilot.

      2Plan it

      1. On Plan, type or paste the brief, or click Attach. You can also drop a file on the box.
      2. Region: leave From the text to use the place your brief names, or choose one.
      3. Planner: the rule-based planner is instant and keeps everything in your browser. To have an AI model write the design, see Use an AI model.
      4. Click Design architecture, or press Ctrl+Enter. The first plan downloads the planning engine, about 15 MB; after that, plans take seconds.
      5. Six steps run: read, understand, design, add build and deploy, describe the workflows and map to every cloud. Show steps lists what was found at each one.

      On clarchy.com, the first time you start you are asked for your name, company and email. Your first 3 diagrams from your own briefs are free; samples, examples and re-planning don't use them. See Pricing.

      TipNo brief yet? Try a sample, under the box, runs a ready-made one.

      3Read the drawing

      • Pick a cloud with the tabs above the drawing: AWS, Azure, Google Cloud or Open source. It is the same design, drawn in each cloud's own style.
      • Click any service to see why it is there: the sentence of your brief that asked for it, how it connects, its sizing, its monthly cost and the alternatives.
      • Actual size shows the drawing at full scale. Esc closes the details.
      • The title block under the drawing lists the cloud, the region and the number of services.

      4Check cost, policies and workflows

      The tabs next to the cloud names show the rest of the design.

      Services
      Every part of the design with the service it uses on this cloud, grouped by stage, and why it is there.
      Cost
      The monthly estimate, and totals for 1 month, 6 months, 1 year and 3 years, on demand and with commitments. Add startup or model credits to see what is left to pay. AWS prices come from the AWS Price List API; Azure, Google Cloud and third-party prices are marked approximate.
      Policies
      The AI and data rules that apply, such as the EU AI Act, GDPR or HIPAA. Each obligation is covered by a component, a gap with a suggestion, or an action for your team. A checklist for design reviews, not legal advice.
      Workflows
      Numbered paths through the drawing: a user's request, a release, a background job. Press Play to walk through one step by step.
      Spec
      The YAML behind the drawing. Edit it and the drawing, services and costs update as you type; Reset brings back the plan.

      5Answer questions and re-plan

      1. The bar above the drawing lists the questions your brief left open and the assumptions Clarchy made instead.
      2. Click Answer and re-plan. Answer in your own words, such as 50k, yes or Frankfurt, or correct an assumption.
      3. Clarchy plans again with your answers. Answered questions are not asked again, and each round keeps the answers you gave before.

      About Clarchy

      Clarchy turns a requirements brief into a cloud architecture you can question, price and compare across AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and open source.

      What it does

      You describe a system in plain language: who uses it and how many, where they are, how much data it keeps and which rules apply. Clarchy turns that into an architecture: the services, how they connect, how changes reach production and the main workflows through it. It draws the same design for AWS, Azure, Google Cloud and open source, so you can compare them side by side, and every service links back to the sentence of your brief that called for it.

      Where the numbers come from

      AWS prices come from the AWS Price List API and are refreshed every week. Azure, Google Cloud and third-party prices are compiled from their public pricing pages and marked approximate. Each estimate lists the sizing it assumes, so you can check it against your own figures. Estimates use list prices: your provider's calculator and your own discounts give the final number.

      What happens to your brief

      On clarchy.com the planner runs in your browser, so your brief and documents are not uploaded anywhere. If you choose an AI model, your brief goes from your browser straight to that model's provider, with your own access token. The privacy page has the details.

      Privacy

      Clarchy is built so that your requirements stay with you. This page explains what Clarchy keeps about you, and what happens to your data when you use clarchy.com.

      Updated 2 October 2026

      Who runs Clarchy

      Clarchy is run by Namrata Bhatia. For any question about your data, email namrata.bhatia@clarchy.com.

      What you tell us when you sign up

      Before your first diagram, Clarchy asks for your name, company and email, and whether you want product updates. It keeps these with the date you signed up, whether you joined the Pro waitlist, how many diagrams you have planned, when you last visited, and a one-way, salted fingerprint of your IP address that is used only to stop automated sign-ups. Your IP address itself is not stored.

      These details are used to give you your free diagrams, to manage your account and the Pro waitlist, and to reply to you. Product updates are sent only if you ticked that box, and you can unsubscribe at any time. Clarchy does not sell or share your details. They are stored in a Cloudflare D1 database and kept until you ask for them to be deleted.

      Your briefs and documents

      With the rule-based planner, your brief and any document you attach are read and planned in your browser. They are never sent to Clarchy, and Clarchy does not store them; only the count of diagrams you plan is recorded.

      If you use an AI model

      If you choose an open-source model through Hugging Face, or another endpoint you enter, your browser sends your brief to that provider together with the access token you paste. That provider's terms and privacy policy apply to the request. The token is kept only for the current browser tab and is never sent to Clarchy.

      What your browser keeps

      Your browser keeps, in its local storage, your account's random identifier and its free-diagram count, your current draft, your model and endpoint choices, and any credits you enter, so you don't have to sign up or retype them again. They stay on your device, and you can clear them at any time in your browser's settings. Clarchy sets no cookies and uses no analytics or advertising trackers.

      Downloads from other services

      The first time you plan, your browser downloads Pyodide, which runs Python in the browser, and its packages from the jsDelivr network. Like any download, this shares your IP address with jsDelivr. Everything else, including fonts, comes from clarchy.com.

      Hosting

      clarchy.com is hosted by Cloudflare. Like any web host, Cloudflare processes technical data such as your IP address and browser type to deliver the site and protect it from abuse, and it may set a strictly necessary security cookie when it checks for automated traffic. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

      Your choices

      To see, correct or delete what Clarchy keeps about you, or to stop product updates, email namrata.bhatia@clarchy.com. If you email Clarchy, your address and message are used only to reply to you.
